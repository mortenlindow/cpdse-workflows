PDS Competence Model

Seven domains → 30 sub-areas → ~123 named competencies. Each competency is rated on an L1–L5 Dreyfus scale; L3 (Proficiency) is the working target. Clients self-rate at the domain level; consultant analysis drills into the sub-areas.

How

Pedagogic Practices

Sharon L. Bowman's Training from the Back of the Room. The person doing the work is the person doing the learning — so our workshops are facilitated, not lectured. Two constructs drive every session.

The 4Cs — how a session is structured

C1
Connections

Anchor learners to what they already know and to each other.

C2
Concepts

Deliver new content in short, multi-sensory bursts.

C3
Concrete Practice

Learners apply it themselves — the heart of retention.

C4
Conclusions

Learners summarise, evaluate, and commit to action.

A cycle, not a line — Conclusions feed the next session's Connections.

The Six Trumps — how each block is run

Movement trumps sitting
Talking trumps listening
Images trump words
Writing trumps reading
Shorter trumps longer
Different trumps same

Self-contained by design

Each model describes only itself — it does not cross-reference the others. How they combine (which competencies matter at which value-chain stage, delivered with which practices) is composed per engagement, not hard-coded into any single model. That keeps each model free to evolve on its own.
