## What this is

The third of CPDSE's three reference models (alongside the
[Pharma Value Chain Model](pharma-value-chain-model/) and the
[PDS Competence Model](competency-model/)). It is the **pedagogical reference
behind all CPDSE workshop and course design**.

The source is the work of **Sharon L. Bowman**:

- ***Training from the BACK of the Room! 65 Ways to Step Aside and Let Them
  Learn*** (Pfeiffer/Wiley, 2009) — the origin of the **4Cs** instructional
  design map.
- ***Using Brain Science to Make Training Stick*** (Bowperson, 2011) — the
  origin of the **Six Trumps**, six brain-based learning principles.
- Companion: *The Ten-Minute Trainer* (2005).

Core premise: **the person doing the work is the person doing the learning.** The
trainer's job is to step aside and design experiences in which learners connect,
discover, practise, and commit — rather than be lectured at. This is why CPDSE
workshops are facilitated, not presented.

---

## The 4Cs — instructional design map

Bowman's 4Cs are a sequence for designing any learning block, from a 10-minute
segment to a multi-day course. Every CPDSE workshop block should be classifiable
as one (or a blend) of the four.

1. **Connections** — Learners connect with what they *already* know about the
   topic, with what they're *about* to learn, with each other, and with their own
   goals for the session. Opens the session and lowers the threat response so the
   brain is ready to learn.
   *Examples:* opening round, "what do you already do about X?", pair warm-ups.

2. **Concepts** — Learners take in new information through **multiple senses**
   (hear it, see it, discuss it, write it, do something with it), delivered in
   **short segments** rather than long lectures. The trainer's "teaching" lives
   here — but kept brief and interactive.
   *Examples:* short framing input, concept maps, interactive walkthrough of a
   diagnosis.

3. **Concrete Practice** — Learners **actively practise or apply** the new
   knowledge/skill themselves. This is learner-led, not trainer-led, and is the
   heart of retention. If only one C is done well, make it this one.
   *Examples:* the gap-card sorting exercise, dot voting, drafting commitments.

4. **Conclusions** — Learners **summarise, evaluate, and commit**. They state
   what they learned, what it means for them, and what they will do next. Closes
   the loop and turns insight into action.
   *Examples:* commitments read-back, action/owner/date table, closing round.

## The Six Trumps — brain-based principles

Six short rules of thumb for *how* to run each block. Each names one thing that
beats another for learning. Use them to pressure-test any activity: if a block
is long, silent, text-heavy, and static, it is fighting the brain.

1. **Movement trumps sitting** — Physical movement raises blood/oxygen flow to
   the brain; moving learners learn more than passively seated ones.
   *Apply:* stand-up sorts, walking to a wall to place sticky notes/votes.

2. **Talking trumps listening** — Learners talking *about* the content beats
   them passively listening to it.
   *Apply:* turn-and-talk pairs before any group discussion; teach-backs.

3. **Images trump words** — The brain processes and remembers visuals far better
   than words alone.
   *Apply:* the value-chain card, capability snapshot, gap cards on the board.

4. **Writing trumps reading** — The physical act of a learner writing embeds
   learning more than reading the same text.
   *Apply:* learners fill in correction zones, gap cards, the commitments table
   by hand.

5. **Shorter trumps longer** — Content in short segments (≈10–20 min) with
   breaks/changes beats long unbroken stretches; spacing aids consolidation.
   *Apply:* the six-block, time-boxed workshop structure.

6. **Different trumps same** — Novelty and variety capture attention; the brain
   notices change and tunes out sameness.
   *Apply:* alternate input → exercise → reflection; vary frame colour/format
   per block.

## How CPDSE uses this

The unifying CPDSE methodology line —
> *"To improve capabilities in PharmaValueChain we train and catalyse Data
> Science Competencies using Training From the Back of the Room practices"*

— is the verbal compression of the three reference models, and this document is
the "Training From the Back of the Room" leg of it.

**4Cs → Workshop 1 block mapping** (the six blocks should map cleanly onto the
4Cs):

| Workshop 1 block | 4C |
|---|---|
| Open + Mirror (validate the profile) | **Connections** |
| Landscape (curated provocations) | **Concepts** |
| Gaps (gap-card exercise, dot vote) | **Concepts + Concrete Practice** — the gap-card exercise is the Concrete Practice anchor |
| Roadmap (filter question, ownership) | **Conclusions** (begins) |
| Close (commitments read-back, handover) | **Conclusions** |

The Six Trumps are the design check applied *within* each block (see "Apply"
notes above) — e.g. the gap exercise uses movement (stand-up sort), images (gap
cards), writing (learners annotate), and talking (challenge-zone discussion).
